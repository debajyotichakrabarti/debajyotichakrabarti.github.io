Overview

This project studies model-based reinforcement learning for quadruped robot soccer. The control stack is hierarchical: a high-level R2-Dreamer policy performs task-level decision making while frozen low-level locomotion policies execute specialized behaviors (dribbling and kicking).

Compared with the original Dreamer, R2-Dreamer removes the image-reconstruction decoder and uses a redundancy-reduction objective to learn compact, task-relevant representations more efficiently while retaining latent imagination for policy learning.

These low-level locomotion skills are trained separately using domain randomization for robust movement and ball interaction, allowing high-level learning to focus on strategy and skill selection.

Iterative Self-Play in Isaac Sim

All current training and evaluation are performed in Isaac Sim; the policies have not yet been tested on the physical quadruped.

Competitive play creates a non-stationary learning problem: as the opponent changes, the behavior that the ego policy must anticipate also changes. Training only against a fixed opponent can therefore produce a policy that does not adapt to progressively stronger competition.

At the first self-play iteration, the opponent follows a random patrolling policy. After the R2-Dreamer ego policy is trained, it is frozen and copied to the opponent for the next iteration. A new ego policy is then trained against this stronger baseline, creating an iterative ladder intended to improve the current policy over time.

Because each iteration faces a different opponent, these values measure adaptation to changing competition rather than performance on one fixed benchmark.

Hierarchical Soccer Policy

The high-level R2-Dreamer policy receives robot, ball, and game state and chooses a task-level command or skill. Frozen low-level policies translate that interface into specialized dribbling or kicking behavior. The skills are trained separately and need not share identical observations or command representations.

Risk-Sensitive World-Model Learning

Performance Study

This study compares the risk-neutral policy with a Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR)-constrained extension of R2-Dreamer. Instead of optimizing only expected return, the additional objective emphasizes lower-tail imagined outcomes corresponding to rare but costly failures.

A central question is whether risk estimates obtained from imagined world-model trajectories are reliable predictors of real closed-loop tail behavior.

Current Research Questions

  • How should a high-level world-model policy coordinate specialized locomotion skills?
  • How does iterative self-play affect competitive strategy and skill selection?
  • When are imagined tail-risk estimates predictive of real closed-loop risk?
  • How does world-model error on rare failure dynamics affect risk-sensitive policy learning?
  • How can these policies transfer reliably from simulation to a physical quadruped?

Ongoing Research

Training and evaluation are ongoing. The comparison above is a current aggregate performance snapshot. All reported results are simulation-only; hardware evaluation remains future work.
