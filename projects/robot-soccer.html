Overview

This project studies model-based reinforcement learning for quadruped robot soccer. The control stack is hierarchical: a high-level Dreamer policy performs task-level decision making while frozen low-level policies execute dribbling and kicking behaviors.

The low-level skills are trained separately using domain randomization for robust locomotion and ball interaction, allowing high-level learning to focus on strategy and skill selection.

Training in Simulation

The policies are trained and evaluated in Isaac Sim before transfer to the physical quadruped. The training traces below show success-rate behavior across the evaluated policy runs.

Hierarchical Soccer Policy

The high-level Dreamer policy receives robot, ball, and game state and chooses a task-level command or skill. Frozen low-level policies translate that interface into specialized dribbling or kicking behavior. The skills are trained separately and need not share identical observations or command representations.

Risk-Sensitive World-Model Learning

Performance Study

This study compares the risk-neutral policy with a Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR)-constrained extension of R2-Dreamer. Instead of optimizing only expected return, the additional objective emphasizes lower-tail imagined outcomes corresponding to rare but costly failures.

A central question is whether risk estimates obtained from imagined world-model trajectories are reliable predictors of real closed-loop tail behavior.

Current Research Questions

  • How should a high-level world-model policy coordinate specialized locomotion skills?
  • When are imagined tail-risk estimates predictive of real closed-loop risk?
  • How does world-model error on rare failure dynamics affect risk-sensitive policy learning?
  • How can these policies transfer reliably from simulation to a physical quadruped?

Ongoing Research

Training and evaluation are ongoing. The comparison above is a current aggregate performance snapshot; additional checkpoints and physical-robot transfer studies are in progress.
