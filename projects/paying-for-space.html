Paying for Space: Incentive-Aware Motion Planning for Multi-Agent Collision Avoidance

Abstract

Advanced Air Mobility (AAM) systems require scalable coordination mechanisms to manage large fleets of aerial vehicles operating in shared, capacity-limited airspace. In such environments, different operators may have private preferences over trajectory characteristics, such as travel time, fuel consumption, or deviation from nominal routes. If centralized traffic management relies on self-reported preferences, operators may strategically misreport their costs to obtain more favorable trajectories.

This paper proposes a multistage motion planning framework augmented with mechanism design to enable collision avoidance for AAM systems with privately known costs. The proposed approach integrates convex safe corridor construction with a VCG-inspired mechanism to ensure conflict-free passage through constrained airspace while incentivizing truthful revelation of private preferences. Simulation results demonstrate safe and decentralized coordination among agents with heterogeneous preferences.

Key Contributions

  • An incentive-aware, VCG-based framework for multi-agent air corridor allocation under collision-avoidance constraints.
  • A bilevel allocation-and-execution formulation that connects centralized airspace allocation with decentralized agent-level trajectory generation, together with a tractable multistage decomposition of the bilevel optimization.
  • A convex safe-corridor construction based on pairwise separating hyperplanes with optimized locations, enabling non-overlapping corridor allocation without mixed-integer scheduling.
  • Simulation studies assessing nominal performance and empirical incentive behavior under truthful and misreported preference reports.

Method Overview

Agents report private cost preferences to a central planner. A three-stage decomposition first computes nominal collision-free trajectories, then solves decentralized per-agent problems to determine convex corridor "mixing" variables that split the shared airspace via pairwise separating hyperplanes, and finally fixes the resulting corridors so each agent can independently plan within its own assigned region. VCG-inspired transfers are computed from the same allocation to incentivize truthful preference reporting. Because the decomposition only approximates the exact centralized VCG allocation, the resulting mechanism is best described as incentive-aware rather than strictly strategyproof.

Results

In a simulated AAM landing-coordination scenario, the proposed method (P4) was compared against two baselines: Stage-1-only centralized planning without corridor sharing (P2), and a fixed 50/50 corridor split (P3). Across 70 Monte Carlo trials with randomized initial conditions and preference profiles, the proposed method achieved higher average social valuation than both baselines, in both truthful and misreporting settings.
